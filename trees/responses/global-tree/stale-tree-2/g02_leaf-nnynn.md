# Degeneracy-breaking for leaf `root_no_no_yes_no_no`

## Physics of this leaf (what the path already tells us)

All 169 units are Complex Scalar Singlet + dark U(1)′ models (both charge signs, both Z_N pools) in a **secluded-annihilation** configuration:

- **⟨σv⟩ is large but DD/ν/h→inv are dead.** The path (σv 10–100× the CTA-WW projection, MDM ≈ 95–316 GeV) puts ⟨σv⟩ ≈ 10⁻²⁵±⁰·⁵ cm³/s, driven by SS* → Z′Z′ with g′ ≈ 0.16–0.32 and MZp ≪ MDM. Direct detection is null for a structural reason: the U(1)′ gauge vertex is *off-diagonal* between the real components (sr–si–Z′), and the portal term α₁H²sr² splits them by δm ≈ α₁v²/2MDM ≈ 0.15–1.5 GeV — so Z′-mediated nuclear scattering is inelastic and kinematically frozen (halo energy transfer ≲ 100 keV), while the surviving elastic Higgs-portal piece has α₁ ≲ 0.01. BR(h→inv) is identically zero since 2·MDM > m_h. Everything about this leaf hangs together as "secluded dark-photon-mediated scalar DM."
- **The annihilation products are fixed by MZp alone, not ε.** Each on-shell Z′ decays through kinetic mixing with dark-photon branching ratios set purely by MZp (even ε = 10⁻⁶ gives cτ ≲ metres — astrophysically instantaneous). This is the key un-used lever: the *cosmic-ray composition* of the annihilation signal.
- **Honest limits of separability.** The [+] vs [−] Lagrangian labels are related by S → S* (dark-charge conjugation) and are physically indistinguishable in principle — no experiment can split them. The Z2 vs Z2+3+4+5 pools differ only in dark-sector quartic self-couplings (α₃ si·sr³, α₅ si³·sr), whose leading observable effect (DM self-interaction, σ/m ~ 10⁻⁵ cm²/g here) is far below cluster bounds. So the *meaningful* degeneracy to break lives in (MZp, MDM, ε) space, and I say so rather than pretending a Lagrangian-level split exists.

## Level 1 (lit review): AMS-02 cosmic-ray antiprotons

The catalog's indirect-detection axes are all photons (Fermi/CTA per channel) and neutrinos (IceCube); **antiprotons are absent and are maximally discriminating here** because of a sharp kinematic threshold: Z′ → hadrons can produce p̄ only if MZp > 2m_p = 1.876 GeV.

Predicted p̄/p excess over the secondary background at 5–50 GeV, region by region (⟨σv⟩ ≈ 10⁻²⁵ cm³/s throughout the leaf; AMS-02 already constrains bb̄-like spectra to ⟨σv⟩ ≲ 3×10⁻²⁶ cm³/s at these masses, arXiv:1610.03071, arXiv:1903.02549):

- **MZp ≳ 10 GeV** (Z′ → bb̄/qq̄ ≈ 60–70%, p̄ multiplicity 1–3 per annihilation): excess ≈ 30–300% of the secondary flux — a signal 3–30× current AMS sensitivity. This covers the bulk of the 149 "seen" regions (e.g. R13, R15, R44, R48, R109, R125, R130 at MZp 30–140 GeV).
- **2 ≲ MZp ≲ 10 GeV** (light-quark/hadronic BR ~ 50%, near-threshold p̄ suppression): excess ≈ 10–50% — still visible, though the softest cases are marginal. Straddler regions whose MZp range dips toward the threshold (R0, R38, R56, R66, R101, R132) are assigned "seen" by their log-mean MZp but flagged marginal.
- **MZp < 1.876 GeV** (exactly the MZp = 1 GeV regions): p̄ yield is *identically zero* — Z′ decays only to e⁺e⁻/μ⁺μ⁻/pions. These 20 regions (R1, R10, R22, R25, R43, R57, R73, R74, R80, R89, R97–R100, R103–R106, R128, R133; R57's MZp reaches 2.7 GeV so it is the one marginal member) predict no excess at any energy. The general cascade formalism for these hidden-sector spectra is arXiv:1511.08787.

This split is ε-independent (annihilation is secluded), gives every region a definite prediction, and — unlike any collider Z′ probe — cannot collide with the catalog's pp → Z′ → ℓℓ recast. **Status: Splits!** (149 vs 20). It does not separate the Lagrangian labels, for the principled reasons above.

## Level 2a (novel, attached to the 149 p̄-producing regions): CTA deep-GC endpoint spectroscopy

Within the hadronic group all spectra are bb̄-like cascades; the remaining physical lever is the kinematic endpoint at E_γ ≈ MDM. Because the leaf sits 10–100× *above* the CTA-WW projected limit, a CTA-South deep GC exposure detects thousands of signal photons — enough to fit the cutoff to ~±10%, i.e. treat the excess as a *measurement*, not a limit ratio (which is all the catalog uses). Cut: cutoff energy ≥ 200 GeV. High-mass side (76 regions, e.g. R13 endpoint ≈ 285 GeV, R109 ≈ 300 GeV, R48 ≈ 260 GeV) vs low-mass side (73 regions, e.g. R18 ≈ 130 GeV, R50/R78/R79 ≈ 137 GeV, R117 ≈ 118 GeV). About 15 regions whose MDM range straddles 200 GeV (R4, R11, R17, R28, R30, R32, R36, R42, R53, R71, R87, R94, R122, R159, R167) are assigned by log-mean and are marginal. Feasibility: CTA-South exists as a funded instrument with ΔE/E ≈ 7% at 100–300 GeV — improvement factor ≈ 1; the dominant systematic is the 2–3% energy-scale calibration plus source-confusion in the GC, neither of which moves a 200 GeV cut materially. Rating: **possible**.

## Level 2b (novel, attached to the 20 leptophilic regions): positron spectral edge with a next-generation magnetic spectrometer

For MZp ≈ 1 GeV, ≥ 60% of Z′ decays are e⁺e⁻/μ⁺μ⁻; SS* → Z′Z′ → 4ℓ at ⟨σv⟩ ≈ 10⁻²⁵ cm³/s produces a near-box positron spectrum with a sharp edge at E ≈ MDM — a flux comparable to the entire observed AMS-02 positron excess above 100 GeV, but with a distinctive edge no pulsar continuum mimics. Cut: e⁺ edge at ≥ 180 GeV. Edge-high regions (MDM log-mean > 180): R1, R10, R22, R43, R80, R99, R100, R103–R106 (MDM ≈ 190–242 GeV). Edge-low: R25, R57, R73, R74, R89, R97, R98, R128, R133 (MDM ≈ 95–181 GeV). Feasibility: AMS-02 has O(10³) positrons total above 100 GeV — too few to resolve an edge; DAMPE/CALET have the statistics but only in e⁺+e⁻. A dedicated successor magnetic spectrometer (AMS-100 class) needs ~3–10× AMS-02's high-energy positron statistics; dominant systematic is the absolute energy scale and the smooth astrophysical positron background under the edge. Rating: **unlikely**.
