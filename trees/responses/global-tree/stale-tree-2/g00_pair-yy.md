# Reasoning

## Physics common to both target leaves

Both leaves sit under the same path prefix: a neutrino signal at IceCube-Gen2 (1–10× limit) **and** a Galactic-centre gamma-ray signal 10–100× the projected CTA (WW) limit. At m_DM ≈ 550–710 GeV the projected CTA WW limit is ⟨σv⟩ ≈ (0.5–1)×10⁻²⁶ cm³/s, so every unit in these leaves predicts **⟨σv⟩ ≈ 10⁻²⁵–10⁻²⁴ cm³/s today** — a bright, *detected* indirect signal, not a limit. The two leaves differ only in whether DARWIN also sees σ_SI (1–10× its limit) or not; that changes nothing about the annihilation final state, which is where the residual discrimination power lives. The units fall into two physically distinct classes:

**Class A — the doublet (R0 in both leaves, CsDh_DM).** An inert-doublet-like complex scalar at 550–620 GeV. Its annihilation products are exclusively electroweak/Higgs bosons: W⁺W⁻, ZZ, and (for the α₄ ≲ 10 corners) hh. All positrons come from soft, broad W/Z/h decay chains: the e⁺ yield above ~100 GeV per annihilation is small and the spectrum carries no hard feature. Expressed as the quantity positron analyses report, the hard "e⁺e⁻-equivalent" cross section is ≲10⁻²⁷ cm³/s even at the top of the leaf's σv band.

**Class B — every other unit (CsSg_U1p[±], all Z_N variants).** A dark-U(1)′ scalar singlet with g′ ≈ 0.3–0.5 and M_Z′ = 1–580 GeV < m_DM. For these points χχ* → Z′Z′ is the dominant channel — indeed it essentially *must* be: the perturbative tree-level σv is ~2×10⁻²⁶ cm³/s, and reaching the observed 10–100× CTA band requires the Sommerfeld enhancement generated by the same light Z′ exchange (α_dark ≈ 0.01–0.02, v ~ 10⁻³ gives S ~ πα_dark/v ~ 30–60), which operates only if the Z′Z′ channel is on. Each Z′ decays through kinetic mixing with photon-like branching fractions — BR(e⁺e⁻) ≈ BR(μ⁺μ⁻) ≈ 0.12–0.15 for M_Z′ between ~1 and ~580 GeV — *independently of ε* (even ε = 10⁻⁶ gives cτ ≲ mm, prompt on astrophysical scales; the Z′ has no lighter dark state to decay to). So every Class-B unit injects **hard box-spectrum positrons**: ~0.3 e⁺ per annihilation with energies up to ≈ m_DM, i.e. an e⁺e⁻-equivalent cross section of ≈ 0.3 × (10⁻²⁵–10⁻²⁴)/2 ≈ **3×10⁻²⁶–3×10⁻²⁵ cm³/s**.

## Level 1 (both leaves): AMS-02 cosmic-ray positron spectrum

Cosmic-ray positrons are **not** in the 18-observable catalog (which carries only γ-ray channels, GC neutrinos, DD, h→inv, LHC topologies, Z′ dileptons). AMS-02 has measured the e⁺ flux from 0.5–1000 GeV; John & Linden (arXiv:2107.10261) turn it into ⟨σv⟩ limits that reach ~10⁻²⁶–10⁻²⁵ cm³/s for hard leptonic channels at 500–700 GeV, and Bergström et al. (arXiv:1306.3983) established the method. Cut in absolute units: **does the AMS e⁺ spectrum contain a hard component corresponding to ⟨σv⟩(e⁺e⁻-equivalent) ≥ 3×10⁻²⁶ cm³/s?** Class B predicts 3×10⁻²⁶–3×10⁻²⁵ (yes — at or above current AMS sensitivity; parts of the band are already in tension, which is itself decisive information). Class A predicts ≲10⁻²⁷ (no — an order of magnitude or more below the cut even at the top of the σv band). This is a *Lagrangian-level* split: it isolates the doublet (the largest single unit in both leaves: 1540 and 18528 points) from all 25 (leaf 1) / 308 (leaf 2) dark-U(1)′ units. Honest caveat: a Class-B corner where the Higgs portal (α₁) dominated annihilation would mimic Class A, but such corners cannot sit in the 10–100×-CTA σv band without the Z′-mediated Sommerfeld boost, so the caveat is confined to the wide catch-all regions' edges. The dominant systematic on the yes-side is the astrophysical (pulsar) interpretation of the GeV–TeV positron excess; the discriminant is robust because the Class-A prediction is far *below* even the astrophysical background.

## Level 2 (both leaves): positron box-edge spectrometry on the residual dark-U(1)′ group

All remaining units share one annihilation topology (χχ* → Z′Z′ → 4f) and differ, observably, mainly in **M_Z′/m_DM** — which is imprinted kinematically on the e⁺ box: for Z′ energy = m_DM, the e⁺ spectrum from Z′→e⁺e⁻ is flat between E₋ = (m/2)(1−β) ≈ M_Z′²/4m and E₊ ≈ m(1+β)/2. Measuring the **lower box edge E₋** in the cosmic-ray positron flux is a genuinely new experimental concept (no existing program targets sub-10-GeV spectral edges as a DM observable) and directly bins the leaves' units by M_Z′:

**Leaf `root_yes_yes_yes`** (cut E₋ ≥ 3 GeV ⇔ M_Z′ ≳ 90 GeV at m ≈ 600–700):
- Light-Z′ group (E₋ ≈ 0.2–1.8 GeV): R1 (M_Z′=36.6 → E₋≈0.5), R5–R11, R13, R15–R18, R20, R21, R24, R25 (M_Z′ ≈ 23–92, mostly 30–77 → E₋ ≈ 0.2–1.8).
- Heavy-Z′ group (E₋ ≥ 3 GeV): R2 (118.7 → 6.3), R4 (69–118 → 3–8.5), R12 (≈86 → ≈3), R19 (68–102 → ≈3), R22 (81–92 → ≈3), R3 (49–193, straddler assigned here by its bulk), and — spectacularly — R14 and R23 (M_Z′ ≈ 562–575, m ≈ 700): β ≈ 0.59 gives a **narrow box from ≈144 to ≈557 GeV**, edges visible at high energy where solar modulation is irrelevant. Straddlers R3, R4, R12, R17, R19, R22 sit within a factor ~2 of the cut; R17 is assigned light. This is stated as a marginality, not hidden.

**Leaf `root_yes_yes_no`** (cut E₋ ≥ 1.5 GeV ⇔ M_Z′ ≳ 55 GeV at m ≈ 500): same observable, cut placed to bisect this leaf's much lighter M_Z′ population. The heavy group (73 regions) contains all M_Z′ ≳ 50–580 GeV units — including the M_Z′ ≈ 90–120 clusters (E₋ ≈ 3–7 GeV), the M_Z′ ≈ 327 clusters (R87, R88, R111, R258: E₋ ≈ 55 GeV), and the M_Z′ ≈ 530–570 clusters (E₋ ≈ 140 GeV) — while the light group (235 regions, M_Z′ from 1 GeV up to ~50 GeV) predicts E₋ ≲ 1.5 GeV, below the practical edge-reconstruction floor. Wide catch-all regions (e.g. R1, R11, R14, R16) span the cut and are assigned by the bulk of their M_Z′ range with lower edges < 40 GeV → light; the assignment impurity for those regions is acknowledged.

**Feasibility (both):** closest instruments are AMS-02 (operating; ~few-% statistical precision on e⁺ at 10–50 GeV, degrading above 300 GeV) and the proposed AMS-100 / ALADInO magnetic spectrometers (~×70–100 acceptance). Resolving a 3 GeV (leaf 1) or 1.5 GeV (leaf 2) spectral edge requires controlling **heliospheric solar modulation** (potential ~0.5–1 GV, the dominant systematic, which distorts exactly the 1–10 GeV range) using time-dependent monitor data, plus next-generation statistics; the high-mass boxes (edges at 55–145 GeV) need only ~×3 more high-energy statistics than AMS-02. Overall a dedicated next-generation effort: rated **unlikely** (~3–10× beyond current capability), not speculative, because the hardware class is already proposed and the high-M_Z′ half of the split is within its reach.

Because the two [+]/[−] U(1)′ builds are charge-conjugate copies of the same dark sector, no experiment can separate the *labels* — only their differently-located parameter regions — which is exactly what the box-edge observable does.
